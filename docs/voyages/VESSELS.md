# Vessels

This document is the synthesis of Nostrarium's completed vessel-experiment
phase. Detailed protocols and raw voyage records live alongside it in this
directory.

## Definition

A vessel is a coherent, named way of moving through the Nostr field on top of
the research engine. A navigator—a human or agent—operates it and makes every
research decision.

A vessel is not an engine feature, a command subset, a UI, or an autonomous
actor. It is a caller-side posture expressed through:

- **movement**: how contact and traversal usually happen;
- **senses**: what evidence is made visible together;
- **judgment**: the conditions and tempo in which the navigator concludes;
- **collection**: what kind of artifact the voyage tries to carry home.

The ownership boundary remains:

```text
engine owns what is evidenced
vessel owns what is attended to
navigator owns what is concluded
```

## What the experiments established

Vessels can change journeys without changing the engine or command vocabulary.
Repeated same-field and reversed-order trials produced stable differences in
movement, observation, judgment, and collection.

Four postures currently behave like complete provisional vessels:

- **Depth** follows one chosen subject into authored evidence and local
  relationships, seeking one explained anchor or rejection.
- **Breadth** keeps several signals and candidates visible, seeking a varied
  lightly qualified set without descending deeply into each account.
- **Skeptic** begins with an explicit reason for interest and actively seeks
  evidence capable of invalidating it before retaining a conclusion.
- **Archivist** declares a bounded dossier, resolves every edge inside that
  boundary, and exposes rather than silently expands everything outside it.

These are useful research postures, not permanent product modes.

## Useful instruments discovered inside proposed vessels

Several experiments revealed strong instruments without yet establishing a
complete vessel:

- **recurrence and concentration** expose dominant authors, exact repetition,
  recurring infrastructure, and distributed repetition;
- **rarity** exposes curiosity pockets after the navigator chooses a meaningful
  dimension;
- **local trail movement** follows one explicit relationship at a time and
  preserves the reason for every step;
- **temporal difference** compares bounded in-session snapshots without
  claiming that difference means globally new, deleted, or exhaustive;
- **conversation traversal** reconstructs threads once a viable conversational
  entrance exists.

These instruments may be used by many vessels. They should not be promoted
into engine conclusions such as “spam,” “coordination,” “human,” “important,”
or “interesting.”

## Incomplete and negative results

- **Correspondent** remains inconclusive. Three blind boardings found isolated
  events, so conversation-only movement was never genuinely tested. The
  failure was boarding, not traversal.
- **Keyhole** proved that locality and trail-shaped collection are valuable,
  but its strict sensory blindness conflicted with the evidence needed to
  evaluate a landing.
- **Dowsing Rod** showed that rarity by itself is empty. Rare values become
  useful only after the navigator decides that the dimension matters.
- **Lighthouse** works within one process, but a cross-session temporal vessel
  is blocked by the deliberately absent persistence boundary.
- **Echo Chamber** is a powerful recurrence sense, but its own trials did not
  require a complete posture around that sense.

These results should remain available as evidence, not be polished into a
catalog of obligatory vessels.

## What the playful experiments changed

The rule-driven **Drift** alternated between the strangest visible door and the
strongest repetition. It was captured by a distributed solicitation campaign:
the same recurrence occupied the landing, its infrastructure, and the broader
semantic tag chosen as an exit. This revealed **echo capture**—recurrence can
become navigational gravity—but the voyage reached a question that a dedicated
recurrence analysis could also have found.

The looser playful voyage worked differently:

1. chance exposed a few unranked entrances;
2. the navigator chose one because it provoked curiosity;
3. explicit evidence-bearing doors supplied movement;
4. a small invalidation check corrected the first interpretation;
5. a second random airlock introduced chance inside the discovered region;
6. the voyage stopped with a bounded trail and question.

That voyage moved from random machine-heavy traffic to:

```text
zucchini joke
→ ordinary garden conversation
→ recurring personal gardening
→ random #gardenstr entrance
→ garlic reference
→ attributed relay hint
→ ActivityPub/Mostr bridge
→ gardening and solarpunk neighborhood
```

This destination was not selected by popularity, a published list, or a
directed topic search. Chance supplied entrances, evidence supplied doors, and
curiosity supplied continuity.

A subsequent suite tested six stranger briefs. It reinforced that playful
framing is most valuable when it creates an entrance and remains falsifiable:

- relay comparison showed radically different bounded fields without
  pretending to prove storage absence;
- rarity led to a genuine but unresolved application event;
- a mirror hunt became synchronized protocol-node archaeology;
- zap receipts exposed a value-flow topology unlike follows or mentions;
- a reply-shape census found its proposed mechanical pattern while rejecting
  its insulting interpretation;
- an account “séance” failed because old metadata and absent recent notes do
  not establish reconstructable abandonment.

The briefs exposed useful instruments, not six additional vessels. Their
detailed evidence is in
[`PLAYFUL-EXPERIMENT-SUITE.md`](./history/PLAYFUL-EXPERIMENT-SUITE.md).

## Current conclusion

We should not define more named vessels yet.

The experiments suggest three different layers that must not be confused:

1. **Vessel posture**: a coherent relationship between movement, senses,
   judgment, and collection.
2. **Research instrument**: a reusable way of exposing recurrence, rarity,
   locality, time, or conversation.
3. **Voyage brief**: the lightweight instructions, bounds, and collection
   intent for one concrete session.

A vessel may use many instruments. A voyage may also combine instruments
freely without proving that the combination deserves a permanent vessel name.

The most promising present pattern is a **curiosity-led random voyage**:

- start from a genuinely random bounded field;
- expose a few unranked entrances rather than the whole field;
- let the navigator choose what feels alive, strange, funny, or confusing;
- follow explicit protocol and provenance doors;
- use analytical instruments only when the encountered evidence calls for
  them;
- reintroduce bounded chance when a new region is reached;
- test emerging interpretations lightly;
- stop with a small evidence-backed trail, dossier, candidate set, or question.

This is not yet a vessel specification. It is the best current hypothesis for
discovering what a playful vessel could become.

## Implications for future work

- Keep vessels entirely caller-side.
- Do not encode automatic direction, ranking, or vessel switching.
- Do not turn every useful sense into a new engine operation.
- Preserve random boarding decisions and abandonments visibly.
- Prefer a small random airlock to either total blindness or full-field
  optimization.
- Treat collection artifacts as important outputs: trails, dossiers,
  candidate sets, invalidation records, recurrence maps, and emergent
  questions.
- Repeat curiosity-led voyages before building a vessel framework or visual
  interface.
- Use playful names to open questions, never as labels already proven by the
  evidence.
- State boarding conditions in evidence terms. In particular, do not build a
  historical-absence story from an old profile unless earlier activity is
  positively observed.
- If the same mechanical arrangement repeatedly helps, encode only that
  arrangement as caller-side controls or templates. Keep interpretation and
  direction with the navigator.
